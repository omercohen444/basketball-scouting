Every figure on this site is computed deterministically from official play-by-play. No number anywhere was produced by a language model. The scouting reports are the one place a model appears, and there it selects, prioritises and interprets evidence that was already computed — it never calculates.

This page is the reference for everything the site displays. If a metric, badge, filter or abbreviation appears anywhere else, it is defined here.

Possessions

Every rate on this site rests on one estimate:

{{ possession_formula }}

{{ possession_note }}

A possession starts when a team gains control of a live ball and ends on a turnover, a made field goal, a final made free throw, or a miss followed by a defensive rebound. An offensive rebound continues the same possession — it never starts a new one. A made basket followed immediately by a qualifying and-one foul also continues the possession, so the free throw attaches to the shot that earned it; that pattern occurs in 729 sequences across 180 of the 182 games.

Consistency

The team page shows how steady each headline metric was from game to game. That is a question about typical nights, so it uses the unweighted per-game distribution rather than the season value: a ten-shot game and a ninety-shot game genuinely do count equally when asking what a typical game looks like.

Computed per metric across the season: games, mean, median, standard deviation (sample, needing at least two games), interquartile range (needing at least four), minimum, maximum, and the coefficient of variation.

Why net rating carries no consistency label. The coefficient of variation is the standard deviation divided by the absolute mean. That only means something on a scale that stays clear of zero. Net rating is a difference and sits near zero by construction, so its coefficient of variation inflates without bound and would mislabel a perfectly solid season as volatile. The metric declares that the measure does not apply and the site shows no label at all for it — only the real game-to-game range. The same declaration is used by the evidence-pack builder, so the site and the scouting reports agree on which metrics have a meaningful consistency figure.

Aggregation

Every value on this site is volume-weighted. Raw components are summed across the games in scope first, and the formula runs once on the totals. A season effective field-goal percentage is the sum of makes over the sum of attempts, never the average of each night's percentage.

The alternative — averaging each game's own ratio — is a different and legitimate statistic, but a distributional one. It answers "what does a typical night look like", which is why the consistency block above uses it and the displayed values do not.

Why a saved scouting report can differ. The scouting reports were generated against the older convention, where a segment value was the unweighted mean of each game's segment value. On thin segments that matters: clutch play runs at roughly seven possessions a game, so the older mean let a two-possession night count as much as a fifteen-possession one. On clutch shooting the two conventions differ by up to twelve points, and eleven of the fourteen teams change league position between them.

The evidence-pack mathematics is deliberately frozen for this release and is not being silently rewritten. The stored reports remain exactly as generated, with their provenance intact; the analytics on this site use the volume-weighted convention its season metrics already used. Both numbers are retained in the artifact so any figure can be reconciled against a report.

Scoring sources

Two different kinds of measure sit under this heading and must not be read the same way.

Partitioning

Two-point, three-point and free-throw scoring partition the total. Every point belongs to exactly one of them, and the three shares sum to 100%. This is the only scoring breakdown on the site drawn as a part-to-whole, and the code that draws it refuses any set of shares that does not sum to one.

Contextual

Points off turnovers, second-chance points and fast-break points overlap each other and overlap the partition above. A fast-break lay-up off a steal is points off turnovers, fast-break points and two-point scoring simultaneously. There is no residual category and inventing one would be a fabrication, so these are shown as independent per-game figures, never stacked and never summed.

Transition

Transition on this site means one thing: the data provider flagged that attempt as a fast break. The flag is present on every one of the season's 24,432 shot attempts, and 11.1% of them carry it.

Independent checks against play-by-play timing the flag was not built from: 95.5% of flagged attempts are the first live-ball attempt of their possession, 94.4% happen within eight seconds of a change of possession, and 88.6% follow a defensive rebound or a live-ball turnover — the two canonical origins.

A false flag never means half-court. It means only that the provider did not classify that attempt as a fast break. This is measured, not cautious: 5.7% of unflagged first attempts happen within four seconds of a change of possession — exactly the timing a genuine break shows. No half-court, set-offence or secondary-transition figure exists anywhere on this site, and none can be derived from this flag.

Turnover types

Turnover rate says how often a team gives the ball away. The turnover types on a team's Profile say what kind. They share no denominator and answer different questions.

The categories are the data provider's own, preserved word for word. No scouting taxonomy is invented on top of them: the source field is unusually clean — ten values, not one null anywhere in the season's 5,205 turnovers — so it is already the trusted representation.

Ten categories is more than a page can read at a glance, so four are displayed and all ten are listed underneath. The four are exhaustive over the ten, which is why they can be drawn as shares of the whole:

Shot geometry Experimental

The provider records a coordinate for every shot. All 24,432 shots in the season have one, and every one of them classifies into a zone — there are no unclassified attempts anywhere in the league.

The taxonomy

Four zones: lane, mid-range, corner three and above the break three. There is no restricted-area split and no left-versus-right corner split; both are deliberately deferred. Rim attempts are a separate flag taken from the play-by-play shot type — dunk, lay-up or alley-oop — and do not depend on coordinates at all.

The official two- or three-point value of every attempt is authoritative and is never overridden by geometry. Coordinates only decide which zone inside the family the box score already recorded.

Validation status

Four structural checks pass across 1,104 shots from eight games and five arenas: distance by shot type is basketball-plausible, orientation is consistent between teams and quarters, and the coordinate-implied two-or-three family agrees with official scoring on 92.2% of attempts outside a small ambiguity band.

The human check is twenty shots from one game. Twenty labelled attempts from a single arena. The untuned rule scored 19 of 20; one mismatch was diagnosed as coordinate-grid quantisation and fixed with a general allowance derived from the provider's own measured grid pitch, after which the set scored 20 of 20 — but the same twenty events both diagnosed and confirmed that fix, so it cannot be reported as unbiased held-out accuracy. A fresh blind sample, spanning several games and testing the corner and mid-range boundaries, is still outstanding.

That is enough to describe where a team shoots from. It is not enough to plot a shot, quote a distance, or place a team in a league order on efficiency inside a zone — so the site does none of those. Zone figures carry no rank and no colour, and are marked experimental wherever they appear.

Shot distance in metres is computed but never displayed. It carries roughly a metre of uncertainty and remains only partially validated.

The pipeline

  1. Play-by-play — the provider's raw action stream, cached locally.
  2. Canonical events — actions parsed into shots, rebounds, turnovers, fouls and free throws.
  3. Possessions — a state machine over that stream, ordered by quarter and action id, never by wall-clock timestamps.
  4. Deterministic analytics — the formulas above, over possession subsets.
  5. League context, splits and segments — ranks, wins-versus-losses, the segment grid.
  6. Evidence pack — a fixed, hash-verified bundle of facts per team.
  7. Evidence Triage agent — chooses which facts matter, from a pre-ranked candidate list.
  8. Tactical Scout agent — turns chosen facts into tactical implications.
  9. Head Scout agent — writes the report.
  10. Deterministic validation and rendering — eighteen rules reject unsupported claims before anything is saved.
  11. Saved report — stored once, with the hash of the pack it came from.
  12. This website and the PDF.
Three things worth being explicit about. Agents never calculate a statistic — every number they see was computed at step 4 and frozen at step 6. Browsing this site never invokes a language model; report generation is the only operation that does, and it is not reachable from any public page. And a report is a snapshot taken when it was generated, not text that updates itself.

The dataset

The 2025-26 regular season: 182 games, 364 team-game records, 14 teams, 26 games each. The build refuses to publish unless all of that is present, so a partial cache fails loudly rather than shipping a league that looks complete.

The local cache also holds 115 games from other competitions — cup, playoffs, preseason, second division, youth and women's fixtures. Those are deliberately excluded from every figure on this site. As a guard against ever loading the wrong population, the build pins the season turnover total at {{ league_turnovers }} and refuses to write if it moves; the full cache would give 8,041.

Also worth knowing

  • One legacy label. A metric in the saved scouting reports is stored under a key that reads "trailing by six or more". The underlying bin actually starts at five behind. Correcting the bin would move the value by up to eight points and invalidate every stored report, so the data is left exactly as generated and the label is corrected on the way out — it displays as Trailing 5+ everywhere a user can see it.
  • Website and reports can differ on thin segments. See aggregation.
  • Clutch is thin by nature. About seven possessions a game. Crossed with wins or losses, no team in the league clears the reliability line, and the site says so on every affected cell rather than in a footnote.
  • Nothing here is a significance test. No p-value is computed anywhere in this project. Effect sizes and league positions are descriptive.
